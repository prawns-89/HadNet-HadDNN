Every number, side by side

The paper's reported tables, and the same quantities re-measured — plus the column the paper does not have: SVD given the same storage budget. HadNet at rank r keeps four factors, 2r(m+n) values, against SVD's r(m+n), so a matched-rank comparison hands it double.

The answer depends entirely on the spectrum

On full-rank data — the paper's Tables 2, 5, 6 — HadNet wins the fair comparison too, and by a widening margin. On images it loses at every rank. Natural and medical images are approximately low-rank, so a truncated SVD is already near-optimal; full-rank data has a flat spectrum where truncation is costly and the r² reach genuinely pays.

So the paper's image and video applications are its weakest case, not its strongest — the opposite of the impression Table 9 gives.
