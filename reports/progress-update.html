The stopping criterion is worth mentioning to the professor directly: because the benchmark scripts inject exactly 0.01·randn, the residual floor σ√N is known in closed form. Predicted 19.32; realised 19.32. The run reaches it at 309 epochs and then spends the remaining 1,691 fitting noise.

Two correctness defects in the released code

  • No GPU synchronisation anywhere. torch.cuda.synchronize() does not appear in the repository, and the timers close before the .item() that would force a sync. The published GPU timings measure kernel enqueue, not execution. Added at 35 sites.
  • Hardcoded device='cuda' in a default argument crashed HadamardDNN_train on CPU-only builds — the repository could not be run at all without a GPU.

Three findings that contradict the paper

Ordered by how well each survives scrutiny. The chips indicate how each should be presented.

The advantage depends entirely on the spectrum Needs framing

On full-rank random data the Hadamard product genuinely pays — at rank 25 it reaches 0.067 against truncated SVD's 0.612, and at rank 40 it is exact. On images the ordering reverses completely, because natural and medical images already have fast-decaying spectra:

Framing: this does not overturn the paper on its own terms. It says the image and video applications in Tables 7 and 9 are the method's weakest case, not its strongest — the opposite of the impression those tables give. The honest claim is about where the method should be aimed, not whether it works.

HadDNN's factor networks are actively harmful Defensible

Each network maps a factor to itself through a 64-unit bottleneck — but the final layer's bias is already full-dimensional, so it alone spans the whole factor space. The 332M upstream weights add a 64-dimensional correction to something already complete.

Monotone in the wrong direction. Freezing 98.5% of the parameters halves the error; widening the bottleneck doubles it again. Plain factors win at every learning rate from 0.3 to 0.001, with 130× fewer parameters. This rules out "the network just needs to be bigger."

The benchmark rewards fitting its own injected noise Needs framing

The scripts add 0.01·randn and then score relative error against the noisy tensor. At rank 25 on video:

Measured againstRel. error
the noisy target — what the paper reports0.01621
the true video0.02361

The reconstruction is closer to the noise realisation than to the real signal. Every comparison at rank 20 and above partly measures noise-fitting, and a method that overfits harder scores better. At rank 10 the ordering reverses, because the model then lacks the capacity to memorise the noise.

Do not claim

Two statements would not survive questioning. "HadDNN is a hardware problem" — it is not; plain factors beat it at every learning rate with 130× fewer parameters. And quoting 176× as a single number — 27× is free, the remainder costs 3.1% clean error. Quote them separately or the whole figure invites doubt.

3

The 35–50 dB target has a ceiling at 40

This is new analysis and it should be settled with the professor before three months go into it.

Tracing the metric through main_video.py: PSNR is computed against frames — the clean frames captured before noise is added — while relative error is computed against A, the noisy tensor. The two columns in the same table score against different ground truths.

The injected noise is 0.01·randn on data scaled to [0,1], i.e. σ = 2.55 in 8-bit units. A method that fits A perfectly reproduces that noise exactly and scores

PSNR = 20 · log₁₀(255 / 2.55) = 40.0 dB

The consequence for the current workplan

Past the noise floor, better optimisation lowers PSNR. Every convergence win — the 6.5× epoch cut, the multi-pass chunking schedule — fits A more completely, which at rank 20 and above means fitting more noise. The speed programme and the dB target are on orthogonal axes, and at high rank they are in tension. There is therefore a PSNR maximum at intermediate rank, and it has not yet been plotted.

Worth agreeing before committing

Above 40 dB this stops being a decomposition result and becomes a denoising one. That is a legitimate and arguably more interesting claim — "the Hadamard structure rejects noise better than truncated SVD at matched storage" — but it is a different paper from the one the speedup work is building toward. Which of the two is the target should be an explicit decision, not a default.

The measurement gap this has already caused

At rank 25 on video, HadDNN needs 4 GB before activations and out-of-memories on the 6 GB card. That is why HadDNN has no rank-25 row in the replication sweep — a hole in the results table caused purely by hardware, not by the method. Chunked streaming was developed partly as a workaround for the same limit rather than as a designed contribution.

The resource request

One clarification first, because it changes what to ask for: cloud object storage does not fix either constraint. The environment must live on a local filesystem, and VRAM is not storage. The useful asks are local disk headroom, or a GPU instance where disk and VRAM come together.

A
Reclaim local disk — free, today
Roughly 9.3 GB is recoverable now: ~/.cache 6.3 GB, trash 1.6 GB, apt cache 1.4 GB. Restores working headroom and unblocks environment rebuilds. Does nothing for VRAM, and the space refills.
B
External SSD for the environment
A durable fix for the disk half at trivial cost. Still leaves every rank-25 HadDNN run out of reach.
C
On-demand cloud GPU, ≥24 GB VRAM — recommended
Fixes both constraints at once and matches the paper's hardware. Individual fits take seconds to minutes (5.78 s chunked, 11.25 s joint, 57 s for the paper's reference run), so this is an hourly instance for a few days of sweeps, not a standing reservation. An L4 or A10 class card with 100 GB disk is sufficient; an A100 is not required.

What the extra VRAM specifically buys

  • Closes the HadDNN rank-25 gap — the one missing cell in the replication table.
  • Hardware parity with the paper, turning "ratios transfer" into a direct absolute comparison.
  • Rank sweeps past 25, needed to locate the PSNR maximum described in §3.
  • Makes chunking a choice, not a necessity — which makes the streaming result a cleaner contribution rather than a workaround.

Open questions to raise unprompted

  • The paper and its code disagree. §4.2 states a linear activation function; the released code ships Linear → LayerNorm → ReLU. Which produced the published numbers is unresolved, and it affects every HadDNN comparison.
  • The baselines are not like-for-like. The released AGD and IGD run 40,000 iterations on numba-CPU against HadNet's 2,000 epochs on GPU. The reported 21× advantage over TAGD is close to the iteration ratio alone.
  • Under-training versus absence. "The optimiser cannot reach the good solutions" and "the good solutions are not there" are different claims, and the fixed 2000-epoch budget does not separate them.
  • Which target — speed or fidelity. Per §3, the two are in tension above 40 dB. Worth resolving now rather than at the write-up.
